Case open

Who served Jerome’s last meal?

Famous food critic Jerome went to the E.R. two nights ago and later died. He had been poisoned.

Detective Tamara Marshall suspects Chef Berhanu, a rival chef furious about one of Jerome’s scathing reviews. She thinks Berhanu followed Jerome to dinner and slipped something into his drink. The restaurant isn’t a suspect, but its security cameras could prove Berhanu was there. First, she needs to know where Jerome ate.

Jerome had just reviewed three restaurants. Medical Examiner Dr. Bea O’Problem tested his stomach contents for lipids, proteins and carbohydrates (glucose and starch), and tested the dish Jerome always ordered at each place.

Keystone challenge Dr. O’Problem also listed the elements and structure of each large molecule she found.

Your goals

  • Explain how macromolecules are built.
  • Use test results as evidence to solve the case.

Word bank

  • Lipids: fats and oils, like butter, cheese, meat fat and olive oil
  • Proteins: found in meat, fish, cheese, eggs and beans
  • Glucose: a simple sugar that tastes sweet; found in fruit, sauces and some vegetables
  • Starch: a complex carbohydrate found in bread, pasta, potatoes and pizza crust

This story is fiction. The restaurants are real local places used for local flavor, and they are witnesses, not suspects.

How macromolecules are built

Macromolecules are polymers: long chains of smaller building blocks called monomers. Try the model.

Four monomers. Each one has an –OH group, an –H, or both at its ends. Press “Link them” to build a polymer.

Where do the atoms come from?

Sugars are made of carbon (C), hydrogen (H) and oxygen (O). Cells reuse those atoms and add other elements to build new molecules.

What this is

The Mystery Meal is a macromolecule investigation for biology. You test food samples, compare them with the evidence and solve the case.
