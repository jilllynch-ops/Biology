How a turn works

  1. A scenario is drawn. That change is the stimulus.
  2. Pick one receptor, one control center and one or two responses from the card bank.
  3. Name the system and the feedback type, then pick the explanation that fits the loop.
  4. Check your loop. You earn up to 6 points: 1 each for the receptor, control center, response, system, feedback type and explanation.

Blood clotting is the one positive feedback example: the response makes the change bigger until the job is done. Every game includes at least one. Watch out for decoy cards. They are wrong on purpose. At the end you'll get a results report and a completion code to turn in.

Talk it over

  1. Choose one loop. How does the response feed back and change the stimulus?
  2. Blood clotting works differently from the other scenarios. How? Why is it positive feedback?
  3. Which decoy card almost fooled you? Why is it wrong?

Check a code

Type the details exactly as they appear on the student's report.

Names and periods are matched without regard to capital letters or extra spaces. A code that doesn't match usually means the score, name or date on the report was changed.

Aligned to PA STEELS 3.1.9-12.C (HS-LS1-3) and Keystone Biology BIO.A.4.2.1. Crosscutting concept: stability and change.

Restart the game?

This game's progress and scores will be lost. You'll go back to the start screen, and your names and class period will stay filled in.
